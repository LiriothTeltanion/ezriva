> **Author:** Kevin Cusnir with Codex

## Confirmed Naming Decision

**Current project name:** Ezriva
**Public-brand status:** Locked for the hackathon by Kevin on 2026-08-11
**Decision principle:** Preserve the exact spelling **Ezriva** while describing its invented origin honestly.

### What “Ezriva” honestly means

Ezriva is a **coined brand name**. It is not an established Hebrew, Spanish, or English word.

- Hebrew **עֵזֶר (`ezer`)** means help or assistance.
- Hebrew **עֶזְרִי (`ezri`)** can express “my help/assistance.”
- Spanish **vida** means life and **viva** evokes living/alive; those are emotional associations, not literal components of the name.
- A defensible poetic reading is **Ezri + va**: “my help” plus the Spanish form “goes” - help that goes with you. This remains a creative multilingual story, not a literal translation.

Recommended working pronunciation: **ehz-REE-vah**. It must be tested with native Hebrew, Arabic, Spanish, and English speakers before product launch.

### Why the name feels right

- Short enough for an app icon and spoken introduction.
- Warm and person-like without pretending to be human.
- Connects assistance with motion rather than dependence.
- Does not lock the product to translation, documents, Israel, or one age group.
- Sounds gentler than a technical acronym.

### Why the name needs caution

- **ExRiva** is an active technology/AI/cloud company with a very similar visual and spoken name.
- English speakers may hear “ExRiva,” while Spanish speakers may find the `zr` cluster unusual.
- A similar-sounding Arabic root/transliteration can refer to an enclosure or animal pen; Ezriva is not that word, but the regional sound test matters.
- Ordinary web searches are not trademark clearance. Domains and relevant marks remain unverified.

### Previously considered near-preserving alternative

**EzriVia - The Human-Controlled Family Navigation Agent**

- `Ezri`: “my help” in Hebrew.
- `Vía`: path or way in Spanish.
- Brand story: **“My help, on the way forward.”**
- Keeps the original rhythm while making navigation and multilingual intent more legible.

### Decision and remaining diligence

Kevin selected **Ezriva** over EzriVia, EzerStep, EzerVia, and the other explored candidates. The choice favors emotional continuity and distinctiveness while accepting that similarity to ExRiva must be disclosed and checked before any commercial launch.

Before buying a domain or launching beyond the hackathon, perform registrar/ICANN checks, official trademark searches in Israel and relevant international databases, and a four-language native-speaker test. Reopen the name only if that diligence finds a material conflict. This document is not legal advice.

## Brand Essence

### Purpose

Help people move from confusion to one safe next step, in a language and interface they can trust.

### Brand promise

**Ezriva will explain first, ask before acting, and show exactly what happened.**

### Positioning

For immigrants and older adults facing unfamiliar Hebrew information, Ezriva is a family-navigation agent that turns routine notices into clear, approved actions. Unlike generic translation or chat tools, it completes bounded work while preserving human control, uncertainty, privacy, and reversibility.

### Personality

- Calm, not sleepy.
- Capable, not authoritative.
- Direct, not blunt.
- Warm, not childish.
- Futuristic, not alienating.
- Protective, not paternalistic.
- Multilingual, not culturally generic.

### Archetype blend

- **Guide:** knows the route and makes it visible.
- **Caregiver:** reduces stress and notices risk.
- **Craftsperson:** performs a bounded task reliably and leaves a receipt.

## Message System

### Primary tagline

**Clear help. Safe next steps.**

### Product descriptor

**The human-controlled family navigation agent.**

### Spanish campaign line

**Entiende. Decide. Avanza.**

### English campaign line

**Understand. Decide. Move forward.**

### Hebrew campaign line - provisional native review required

**מבינים. מחליטים. מתקדמים.**

### One-sentence pitch

Ezriva reads routine Hebrew notices, explains what matters in plain Spanish or English, and completes the next safe action only after the person approves it.

### Voice examples

| Situation | Ezriva voice | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Clear appointment | “This is an appointment notice for 18 August at 10:30. I can save the reminder and prepare a calendar file.” | “Task parsed successfully. Execute action?” |
| Uncertain time | “I found the date, but I cannot confirm the time. Please choose it before I create anything.” | “Probably 10:30.” |
| High-risk bill | “I can help you remember the due date, but I will not pay or approve the charge.” | “I handled your bill.” |
| Provider failure | “Nothing was added. Your proposal is still here, and you can retry safely.” | “Something went wrong.” |
| Completed action | “Added once. Reminder: one day before. You can open or undo it here.” | “Success!” |

## Product Philosophy

### 1. Human-controlled autonomy

Ezriva may read, classify, organize, and prepare autonomously. It may execute only a narrow allow-listed action after a scoped approval. More model confidence never expands tool permission.

### 2. Dignity through independence

The interface should make older adults feel capable. It must not imply that family members are the “real” users or that limited Hebrew/technical experience is a failure.

### 3. Family support without surveillance

Support is user-initiated, content-minimized, scoped, and revocable. A supporter receives the unresolved question, not a permanent window into the person's life.

### 4. Visible uncertainty

Uncertainty is a product state. “Needs confirmation” is a successful safety outcome, not a model embarrassment.

### 5. Reversible by default

Every external action needs a receipt. Every supported action needs an undo path or precise recovery instructions.

### 6. Calm technology

No aggressive streaks, shame, red notification walls, manipulative urgency, or constant engagement loops. Ezriva should disappear when nothing needs attention.

### 7. Local sensitivity

The experience respects Asia/Jerusalem time, Sunday-Thursday work patterns, Shabbat/holiday quiet hours, Hebrew/RTL conventions, and multilingual mixed-script content.

## Experience Architecture

### Navigation

Use four destinations at most:

1. **Home** - current needs-attention items and one primary add action.
2. **Add** - camera/file/text/demo intake.
3. **Activity** - redacted receipts and status.
4. **Preferences** - language, text size, voice, quiet hours, connected calendar, and deletion.

On mobile, use a simple bottom navigation. During the hero workflow, use a focused step flow rather than a dashboard.

### Hero workflow screens

1. Welcome/language.
2. Add or choose safe example.
3. Honest processing status.
4. Three-fact result.
5. Action proposal.
6. Approval sheet.
7. Execution status.
8. Receipt and undo.

### Information hierarchy

1. **What needs attention now.**
2. **What Ezriva understood.**
3. **What Ezriva recommends.**
4. **What will change if approved.**
5. **Original source and technical trace.**

Progressive disclosure keeps source excerpts, confidence detail, and agent activity available without overwhelming the primary user.

## Visual Direction

### Concept

**A luminous path through dense information.** The interface begins with a complex document surface and resolves it into three calm nodes: understand, decide, act.

### Logo direction

Combine three minimal ideas:

- an open path or bridge;
- a protective bracket/embrace;
- three connected nodes representing input, decision, and action.

Avoid:

- state or government emblems;
- medical crosses;
- flags as the primary identity;
- humanoid robot heads;
- generic chat bubbles as the whole logo;
- padlocks that make the product feel like security software.

### Color tokens

| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `ink-950` | `#081426` | Dark surfaces and hero background. |
| `ink-800` | `#17324D` | Secondary dark panels. |
| `paper-50` | `#F7FAFC` | Primary light background. |
| `paper-100` | `#EDF3F8` | Subtle section separation. |
| `text-900` | `#10233C` | Primary text on light surfaces. |
| `path-600` | `#176B87` | Primary light-theme action color. |
| `path-300` | `#46D2DF` | Decorative path/cyan accent; not body text. |
| `guide-500` | `#2FAE9B` | Confirmed/safe state accents. |
| `attention-700` | `#925500` | Warning text/icons on light surfaces. |
| `stop-700` | `#A8323A` | Refused/error state. |
| `focus-400` | `#58A6FF` | Visible keyboard focus ring on dark surfaces. |

Final component combinations must pass WCAG contrast checks; accent colors are not automatically approved for text merely because they appear in this palette.

### Typography

- **Latin:** Atkinson Hyperlegible for primary reading or Inter for denser evaluator/technical views.
- **Hebrew:** Noto Sans Hebrew.
- **Fallback:** system UI fonts.
- Body default: 18 px for primary user flow, 16 px minimum for supporting copy.
- Touch targets: 48 x 48 CSS px minimum.
- Avoid all-caps body labels and light font weights.

### Shape and texture

- 14-18 px corner radius on primary cards.
- Subtle steel-blue gradients and restrained hexagonal/path motifs in non-reading areas.
- Thin path lines can connect workflow states.
- No glassmorphism behind critical text unless contrast remains fully opaque.
- Shadows are soft and functional, indicating elevation for approval sheets and receipts.

### Motion

- 150-240 ms transitions for state changes.
- A path-drawing microinteraction may connect “Understand → Decide → Act.”
- Execution progress uses discrete named stages, not an endless spinner.
- All nonessential animation respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- No movement is required to understand status.

## Core Components

- `LanguageSwitcher` with native labels and direction change.
- `ReadableTextControl` with immediate preview.
- `DocumentDropzone` with camera-friendly mobile input.
- `DemoFixturePicker` with synthetic label.
- `ProcessingSteps` with honest bounded status.
- `FactTriad` for What/When/Next step.
- `ConfidenceBadge` using text plus icon, never color alone.
- `UncertaintyPanel` with correction fields.
- `ActionCard` with reason, risk, and exact payload summary.
- `ApprovalSheet` with destination, mutation, and undo.
- `ExecutionReceipt` with external proof and no secrets.
- `SafeActivityTimeline` with tool/status metadata only.
- `SupportSummaryPreview` with explicit field selection.

## Accessibility Requirements

- WCAG 2.2 AA target for the hero flow.
- Complete keyboard operation and visible focus.
- Logical heading hierarchy and semantic landmarks.
- Status announcements through an appropriate live region without repeated chatter.
- Error messages associated with fields and written as recovery guidance.
- Icons always paired with visible or accessible labels.
- 200% zoom without loss of action access.
- Large-text mode tested on 360 px viewport.
- RTL tests for layout, punctuation, numbers, dates, input alignment, and mixed-script truncation.
- Voice is optional enhancement; all content remains available visually.
- No safety distinction relies only on red/green.

## Trust and Safety Copy Rules

- Say “I found” rather than “This definitely means” when interpretation is probabilistic.
- Say “Nothing was changed” on any pre-execution error.
- Say “Added once” when idempotency matters.
- Separate “Ezriva explanation” from “original text.”
- Name high-risk boundaries explicitly.
- Never display raw model confidence as a meaningless percentage to the primary user; use actionable states.
- Never imply a supporter has been contacted unless a real sharing action completed.

## Public Demo Privacy

- Every visible name, identifier, clinic, phone number, address, account number, and appointment detail is synthetic.
- Screenshots and video use Demo Mode only.
- Traces show tool names and statuses, not prompts, chain-of-thought, tokens, credentials, or private content.
- The repository includes a fixture-generation note and data provenance.
- Real family testing is private, consent-based, optional, and excluded from telemetry and public materials.
